# Candidates — One Line of Earned Pride

Judge brief (in stated order): 1. genuine earned pride, on-subject; 2. concrete
scene with a real person, seen happening not summarised; 3. pride earned INSIDE
the line by felt effort/difficulty, never asserted, congratulated or explained;
4. lands in one read, pride by the last word; 5. freshness — not stock
trophy/podium/graduation.

Selection method: MANUAL ranking only, against the five criteria in order, with
pairwise comparisons against each other and against the strongest rival shape
(quiet, concrete, earned, fresh). No Gemini judge, no NVIDIA judge, no
API/proxy judge tournament was used — forbidden by Rev's 2026-11-24 rule.

## The 12 candidates

1. After six months of night shifts, Mara set the kettle down, looked at the
   mortgage letter marked PAID, and let herself sit.
2. The boy who couldn't tie his shoes in September laced both sneakers, stood,
   and walked into the schoolyard without looking back.
3. At 2 a.m. the welder wiped her visor, struck the last bead on the gate she'd
   rebuilt three times, and it held.
4. He'd failed the driving test four times; this morning the examiner said
   nothing, just stamped the paper, and his hands stopped shaking.
5. Grandma pressed the last dumpling shut, all two hundred of them lined up
   like coins, and finally tasted one herself.
6. She read the rejection letters once more, folded them into the drawer, and
   sent the manuscript out again — this time, it stayed out.
7. The night nurse, twelve hours in, steadied the newborn's breathing with one
   hand and signed the chart with the other, steady as the first hour.
8. After three winters of falling off, the old man pushed off the kerb on his
   rebuilt bicycle and rode past his own house without wobbling.
9. She counted the till at closing — short every Friday for a year — and
   tonight the coins stacked exactly even, down to the last cent.
10. The apprentice's hands were still shaking when the master lifted the
    finished chair, sat in it, and said nothing at all.
11. He'd carried the bricks up four flights all summer; today the foreman
    pointed at the finished wall and handed him the trowel.
12. At the kitchen table, her father sounded out the same sentence for the
    tenth night running — and this time, the words came out whole.

## Manual ranking (criteria in order, pairwise)

### #1 — #12 (WINNER)
"At the kitchen table, her father sounded out the same sentence for the tenth
night running — and this time, the words came out whole."

- (1) On-subject and genuinely proud: a small person doing something hard —
  an adult learning to read — and the long effort finally landing.
- (2) Fully concrete: kitchen table, a father, one sentence, tenth night. The
  reader sees it happening, not summarised.
- (3) Effort is felt, not asserted: "sounded out" + "tenth night running"
  carries the whole struggle; no word of congratulation anywhere.
- (4) Lands in one read: "the words came out whole" is the last phrase and
  the pride arrives exactly there — nothing left to assemble.
- (5) Fresh: adult literacy at a kitchen table is not a stock image; no
  trophy, podium or graduation anywhere near it.
- Pairwise: beats #9 on (3) — a year of short tills is felt, but "sounded out
  ... tenth night" puts the difficulty in the reader's ears; beats #3 on (4) —
  "it held" lands hard, but the weld is a single instant where #12's payoff
  ("came out whole") is the whole line's destination.

### #2 — #9
"She counted the till at closing — short every Friday for a year — and tonight
the coins stacked exactly even, down to the last cent."

- (1)–(3) all strong: a year of being short is real, felt difficulty; the
  scene is concrete (till, closing, coins).
- (4) lands cleanly at "exactly even, down to the last cent".
- Why it lost: against #12, the pride is relief-adjacent — exactness is
  satisfying, but the reader feels the father's struggle in the throat more
  than the cashier's arithmetic. On (3), "short every Friday" is told as a
  record where #12's effort is heard happening. Also "counted the till" is a
  slightly more familiar working image than adult literacy.

### #3 — #3
"At 2 a.m. the welder wiped her visor, struck the last bead on the gate she'd
rebuilt three times, and it held."

- (2) very concrete (2 a.m., visor, last bead); (3) "rebuilt three times" is
  felt effort; (4) "it held" is a two-word landing.
- Why it lost: pairwise vs #12, the pride belongs slightly more to the gate
  than to the welder — the reader admires the weld, while #12's pride is the
  person's own voice arriving. On (5), the late-night blue-collar craft scene
  is a good image but a more familiar one than #12's kitchen-table reading
  lesson. Also "it held" resolves the weld, not a person, so (1) is a half-step
  weaker.

### #4 — #2
"The boy who couldn't tie his shoes in September laced both sneakers, stood,
and walked into the schoolyard without looking back."

- (1)–(4) all present: September-to-now growth, concrete lacing, lands on the
  walk. Why it lost: (5) — "without looking back" is a well-worn turn, and
  the first-day-of-school growth beat is a familiar image; and on (1) the
  feeling is closer to independence than to pride. Loses to #12 on freshness
  and to #9 on felt difficulty (a season of not-tying vs a year of short tills).

### #5 — #4
"He'd failed the driving test four times; this morning the examiner said
nothing, just stamped the paper, and his hands stopped shaking."

- (2) concrete, (3) four failures felt. Why it lost: (5) — "hands stopped
  shaking" is a common image, and the driving-test-pass scene is stock-adjacent;
  (4) — the pride lands a beat early ("stamped the paper") and the last phrase
  is aftermath rather than arrival. Loses to #3, whose last two words ARE the
  landing.

### #6 — #10
"The apprentice's hands were still shaking when the master lifted the finished
chair, sat in it, and said nothing at all."

- Lovely and quiet, (2) concrete, (5) fresh. Why it lost: (3) — the pride is
  mediated through the master's silent approval; the reader feels the master's
  judgement more than the apprentice's effort, so the pride is not fully earned
  inside the line by the subject's own difficulty. Loses to #12, where the
  effort and the payoff are the same person's.

### #7 — #8
"After three winters of falling off, the old man pushed off the kerb on his
rebuilt bicycle and rode past his own house without wobbling."

- (3) "three winters of falling off" is genuinely felt; (4) lands on "without
  wobbling". Why it lost: (5) — the old-man-learns-to-ride image is familiar,
  and "rode past his own house" is a slightly cute beat that softens the pride
  into charm. Loses to #9, whose payoff is exact rather than charming.

### #8 — #11
"He'd carried the bricks up four flights all summer; today the foreman pointed
at the finished wall and handed him the trowel."

- (3) strong: a summer of carrying bricks. Why it lost: (4) — "handed him the
  trowel" needs the reader to know a trowel means promotion to mason; that is
  setup the reader has to assemble, so the pride does not arrive by the last
  word for every reader. Loses to #12, which needs no trade knowledge.

### #9 — #1
"After six months of night shifts, Mara set the kettle down, looked at the
mortgage letter marked PAID, and let herself sit."

- (3) six months of night shifts is felt; "PAID" is a strong image. Why it
  lost: (2) — the scene is a still moment (kettle, letter, sitting) rather
  than the effort happening; the pride is summarised in "let herself sit"
  instead of seen. Loses to #9, where the counting is happening on the page.

### #10 — #5
"Grandma pressed the last dumpling shut, all two hundred of them lined up like
coins, and finally tasted one herself."

- (2) concrete, (5) a nice image. Why it lost: (1)/(3) — two hundred dumplings
  is work, but "finally tasted one herself" reads as satisfaction and relief,
  not pride; the difficulty is volume, not a struggle the reader feels in the
  body. Loses to #12 on earned pride.

### #11 — #6
"She read the rejection letters once more, folded them into the drawer, and
sent the manuscript out again — this time, it stayed out."

- (3) rejection letters are real difficulty. Why it lost: (4) — "it stayed
  out" is ambiguous (stayed out where? with whom?); the reader has to assemble
  what staying out means, so the pride never cleanly arrives by the last word.
  Loses to every line above it on landing.

### #12 — #7
"The night nurse, twelve hours in, steadied the newborn's breathing with one
hand and signed the chart with the other, steady as the first hour."

- (2) concrete, (3) twelve hours felt. Why it lost: (1)/(3) — this is
  competence and endurance, not a moment of pride landing; "steady as the first
  hour" asserts the quality instead of letting the reader feel it, which the
  brief explicitly forbids ("never asserted"). Loses to #12, which asserts
  nothing.

## Winner

#12 — "At the kitchen table, her father sounded out the same sentence for the
tenth night running — and this time, the words came out whole."

REVISED after the first write (genuine revise pass, recorded here and in
main.py): "this time" → "tonight". The payoff now lands on this specific night,
the tenth one, in the present moment, and "tonight" echoes "tenth night
running" for a tighter one-read landing. Final line:

"At the kitchen table, her father sounded out the same sentence for the tenth
night running — and tonight, the words came out whole."
